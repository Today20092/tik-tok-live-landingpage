# Purpose of life: research for the pamphlet section

Researched 2026-11-21. Qur'an wording is *The Clear Quran* (Mustafa Khattab, quran.com translation 131). Hadith wording is the sunnah.com English. Every quote below was read on the linked page.

## Short answer

Mainstream Sunni teaching: people were created to worship God (51:56). "Worship" (*ibadah*) is wider than ritual. Ibn Taymiyyah's standard definition covers everything God loves and is pleased with, in words and deeds, inward and outward. The Qur'an also frames life as a test, with accountability afterward. The draft's three-way split (God, others, yourself) is well supported: Salman's advice, approved by the Prophet, names the rights of your Lord, your self, and your family, and Yaqeen Institute teaches the same three domains. The draft is accurate. The risks are overstating "all of life" and letting "worship" be heard as ritual only.

## Findings

### 1. Qur'an 51:56 and the exegetes

- Verse: "I did not create jinn and humans except to worship Me." ([Quran.com 51:56, Clear Quran](https://quran.com/51:56?translations=131))
- Ibn Kathir (abridged): God created creatures "so that they worship Him Alone without partners"; the obedient are rewarded and the disobedient punished. He stresses God does not need them (51:57). He reports Ibn Abbas on the phrase as "willingly or unwillingly". ([Ibn Kathir on 51:56](https://quran.com/51:56/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir))
- Al-Qurtubi (Arabic) lists the views. Some say the verse is specific to believers (al-Dahhak, al-Kalbi, al-Farra'; a reading "from among the believers" is attributed to Abdullah). Ali: "to command them to worship". Ibn Abbas (via Ali ibn Abi Talha): affirm worship "willingly or unwillingly". Mujahid: "except to know Me" (*illa liya'rifuni*), which al-Tha'labi calls a good view. ([Qurtubi on 51:56](https://quran.com/51:56/tafsirs/ar-tafseer-al-qurtubi))
- Al-Baghawi gives the same set, says Mujahid's "to know Me" is the best of them, and attributes the "from the believers" reading to Ibn Abbas. ([Baghawi on 51:56](https://quran.com/51:56/tafsirs/ar-tafsir-al-baghawi))
- Ma'arif al-Qur'an raises the same puzzles and notes some limit the verse to believers. ([Ma'arif on 51:56](https://quran.com/51:56/tafsirs/en-tafsir-maarif-ul-quran))

Correction to the brief: in the sources I read, "to know Me" is attributed to Mujahid, not Ibn Abbas. Ibn Abbas is cited for "willingly or unwillingly" and for the "from the believers" reading. Do not pair "Ibn Abbas" with "to know Me" in print.

Disagreement: exegetes differ on whether the verse is universal or about believers, and whether "worship" here means obey, affirm, or know. The plain universal reading in Ibn Kathir is the mainstream default and is what the pamphlet should state.

### 2. Ibn Taymiyyah's definition

- Arabic: "العبادة هي اسم جامع لكل ما يحبه الله ويرضاه من الأقوال والأعمال الباطنة والظاهرة".
- English, as commonly translated: "Worship (al-'Ibadah) is a comprehensive term covering everything that Allah loves and is pleased with - whether saying, or actions, outward and inward." Cited to *Majmu' al-Fatawa* 10/149. ([SunnahOnline](https://sunnahonline.com/library/purification-of-the-soul/624-meaning-of-worship-the))
- A modern commentary on *al-'Ubudiyyah* quotes the same wording as the treatise's definition of worship. ([Shamela, Sharh Risalat al-'Ubudiyyah](https://shamela.ws/book/36581/10))
- Caveat: I did not open a scan of *al-'Ubudiyyah* itself. If a source is named anywhere, cite *Majmu' al-Fatawa* 10/149. The pamphlet does not need to name him.

### 3. Qur'anic framing (Clear Quran wording)

- 2:30: "I am going to place a successive ˹human˺ authority on earth." The Clear Quran says "successive authority", not "vicegerent" or "steward", so avoid "steward" in print. ([2:30](https://quran.com/2/30?translations=131))
- 67:2: "˹He is the One˺ Who created death and life in order to test which of you is best in deeds." ([67:2](https://quran.com/67/2?translations=131))
- 21:25: "There is no god ˹worthy of worship˺ except Me, so worship Me ˹alone˺." ([21:25](https://quran.com/21/25?translations=131))
- 99:6-8: "On that Day people will proceed in separate groups to be shown ˹the consequences of˺ their deeds. So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it. And whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it." ([99:6-8](https://quran.com/99/7?translations=131))
- 3:185: "Every soul will taste death. And you will only receive your full reward on the Day of Judgment. Whoever is spared from the Fire and is admitted into Paradise will ˹indeed˺ triumph, whereas the life of this world is no more than the delusion of enjoyment." ([3:185](https://quran.com/3/185?translations=131))
- 2:2: "This is the Book! There is no doubt about it - a guide for those mindful ˹of Allah˺". ([2:2](https://quran.com/2/2?translations=131))
- 16:89: "We have revealed to you the Book as an explanation of all things, a guide, a mercy, and good news for those who ˹fully˺ submit." ([16:89](https://quran.com/16/89?translations=131))

### 4. Hadith for the branches

- Bukhari 1968 (Salman and Abu al-Darda): Salman said, "Your Lord has a right on you, your soul has a right on you, and your family has a right on you; so you should give the rights of all those who has a right on you." The Prophet said, "Salman has spoken the truth." Context: Abu al-Darda was fasting by day and praying all night. Numbering verified (Book of Fasting; in-book 30:75). ([sunnah.com/bukhari:1968](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1968))
- Bukhari 5199 (to Abdullah ibn Amr): "Observe the fast sometimes and also leave them (the fast) at other times; stand up for the prayer at night and also sleep at night. Your body has a right over you, your eyes have a right over you and your wife has a right over you." Numbering verified. ([sunnah.com/bukhari:5199](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5199))
- Bukhari 1: "The reward of deeds depends upon the intentions and every person will get the reward according to what he has intended." ([sunnah.com/bukhari:1](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:1))
- Muslim 1009 and Bukhari 2989: "Sadaqa is due on every joint of a person, every day the sun rises. Administering of justice between two men is also a Sadaqa ... and a good word is a Sadaqa ... and removing of harmful things from the pathway is a Sadaqa." (Muslim 1009.) Bukhari 2989 is parallel and adds "as a sign of gratitude to Allah". ([Muslim 1009](https://sunnah.com/muslim:1009), [Bukhari 2989](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:2989))

These support the branches and the claim that everyday good counts. They do not say every act is worship regardless of intention; intention (Bukhari 1) is the condition.

### 5. How contemporary sources present it

- Yaqeen Institute, *The Spiritual Psychology of Worship*: "'Ibadah encompasses one's entire life's journey." The five pillars "represent the different domains of our life": knowledge (shahada), "relationship with Allah" (salah), "relationship with the creation" (zakah), "relationship with our nafs" (sawm), and life and death (hajj). This matches the God / others / yourself columns. ([Yaqeen paper](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/the-spiritual-psychology-of-worship))
- Yaqeen, *Basics of Islam*: Islam "clarifies the purpose of creation and guides us on how to live a godly, righteous life"; prayer "fulfills humanity's purpose of worship". ([Yaqeen: Basics of Islam](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/what-islam-says-about/basics-of-islam))
- Yaqeen's wider material (as summarized in search; I did not open the Elshinawy pieces) says worship is for human benefit, not God's need, matching Ibn Kathir on 51:57. ([Yaqeen: Spirituality](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/what-islam-says-about/islam-and-spirituality))

## Pitfalls and caveats

- **"Worship" heard as ritual only.** Christian readers may think of church services. "Worship includes all good done for Him" addresses that.
- **Do not claim every action is worship.** Say "includes" or "can be"; intention is the condition. Exegetes also dispute the scope of 51:56.
- **Do not print "to know Me" with Ibn Abbas.** See section 1.
- **"Learning" in the "yourself" column.** I did not verify a verse or hadith for it, so the versions below use "Health, rest" (Bukhari 5199) and "Self-control" (the pamphlet's own wording; I did not verify a text for it). Add "learning" only after checking a source.
- **Heaven and hell.** 3:185 names Paradise and the Fire; 99:6-8 is about deeds being shown. Match the verse to the claim.
- **Not verified:** al-Tabari on 51:56 (the quran.com page did not render), a scan of *al-'Ubudiyyah*, the Elshinawy Yaqeen articles.

## Recommendation: Version A

- It keeps the author's structure and the three original headers.
- "Worship includes all good done for Him" answers the ritual-only misreading and respects the intention condition (Bukhari 1).
- "After death" avoids the jargon "hereafter", and 99:7-8 directly says deeds are shown down to an atom's weight.
- Every example line is backed above or is a plain virtue; "learning" is dropped as unverified.
- The body is exactly 75 characters, at the limit. If it wraps badly, use the safe variant "God made us to worship Him (51:56). Worship takes many forms:" (61).

Version B is warmer for a conversational tone and brings in heaven and hell via 3:185. Version C is shortest but gives each column only one example.
