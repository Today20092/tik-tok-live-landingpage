## 3. Inside left · What Muslims believe

**Six articles of faith.** Muslims believe in (Sahih Muslim 8):

**Graphic: icon grid.** One God (eight-point star): Allah: Arabic for God · Angels (rays of light): God’s unseen servants · Scriptures (open book): From Torah to Qur’an · Prophets (path of milestones): From Adam to Muhammad · The Last Day (scales): Judgment after death · Divine decree (pen): All within God’s will.

**The prophets and the Qur’an.** All the prophets taught one message: worship God alone (21:25). The last was Muhammad (33:40).

**Graphic: prophets strip.** Abraham · Scrolls, Moses · Torah, David · Psalms, Jesus · Gospel, Muhammad · Qur'an (terracotta).

**The purpose of life.** God made us to worship Him (51:56). Worship includes all good done for Him:

**Graphic: three columns.** For God: Prayer, fasting / Remembering God · For others: Mercy, justice / Honesty, charity · For yourself: Health, learning / Self-control. Footer: **Paradise or Hell follows this life (3:185).**

## 4. Inside center · The Five Pillars

Each Pillar has an icon: a raised index finger, a prayer mat, a hand under a coin, a crescent and star, and the Kaaba.

- **Declaration of faith (*shahada*).** Affirm belief in one God and in Muhammad as God's messenger.
- **Prayer (*salat*).** Pray five times a day, after a ritual washing called *wudu* (5:6).
  **Graphic: the sun through the day.** Fajr · Dawn, Dhuhr · Midday, Asr · Afternoon, Maghrib · Sunset, Isha · Night.
- **Giving (*zakat*).** Muslims with savings above a set amount give 2.5% a year, mainly to people in need (9:60).
- **Fasting (*sawm*).** During Ramadan, Muslims who are able go without food and drink from dawn to sunset.
- **Pilgrimage (*hajj*).** Travel to Mecca once in a lifetime if physically and financially able.

## 5. Inside right · Jesus and Mary in Islam

Muslims love and honor Jesus, called *Isa* in Arabic. The Qur’an tells much of his story:

**Graphic: timeline.** A vertical spine with seven events, each with its verse. The last step is dashed and in terracotta because it is still to come.

- **Mary, chosen over all women** (3:42). The only woman named in the Qur’an.
- **Announced as the Messiah** (3:45). Called a Word from God.
- **Born to a virgin mother** (19:20).
- **Heals the blind and the leper** (3:49). And raises the dead, by God’s will.
- **Given the Gospel (Injil)** (5:46). Guidance and light.
- **Raised up to God** (4:158).
- **Will return to earth** (43:61). A sign that the Last Hour is near.

**How Muslims see Jesus.** (Moss box.) A great prophet and the Messiah, but human, not God or the Son of God (4:171). Muslims believe God raised him up and he was not crucified (4:157–158).

**Read about Jesus and Mary in Surah Maryam, chapter 19.**

## 6. Back · Questions you may have

**Does Islam allow violence against innocent people?** No. The Qur’an says that whoever saves a life, “it will be as if they saved all of humanity” (5:32). It also says, “Let there be no compulsion in religion” (2:256), and calls for justice even toward enemies (5:8).

**What is Sharia?** Sharia is guidance for all of life, from prayer to business. Muslims interpret it differently. The First Amendment protects religious freedom.

**Women's rights in Islam**

- Equal reward for good deeds (3:195)
- Seeking knowledge (Ibn Majah 224)
- Consent to marriage (Bukhari 5136)
- A marriage gift paid to her (4:4)
- An inheritance share (4:7)
- Fair treatment (4:19)
- Seeking a divorce (2:229)

## Extended FAQ for the pamphlet landing page

### Do Muslims worship Muhammad?

Muslims worship God alone. They believe Muhammad received the Qur'an and look to his life for an example of how to practice Islam. The Qur'an describes him as a messenger and the final prophet, and reminds readers that messengers came before him. Muslims often say "peace be upon him" after his name as a sign of respect. [Qur'an 33:21](https://quran.com/33/21), [33:40](https://quran.com/33/40), [3:144](https://quran.com/3/144), [33:56](https://quran.com/33/56), and [112](https://quran.com/112).

### Can people of other faiths live and worship freely?

The Qur'an says, "Let there be no compulsion in religion" (2:256, *The Clear Quran*). It calls on Muslims to act justly even toward people they dislike (5:8) and to treat those who have neither fought nor expelled them with kindness and fairness (60:8). Jews, Christians, and other communities lived and worshiped under various Muslim governments, with rights and restrictions that differed by place and period. For example, an Ottoman reform edict in 1856 reaffirmed protections for religious communities and called for fair representation in communal councils. The edict describes what the government promised. It does not tell us how officials carried out those promises. [Qur'an 2:256](https://quran.com/2/256), [5:8](https://quran.com/5/8), [60:8–9](https://quran.com/60/8-9), and [Ottoman Reform Edict of 1856](https://anayasa.gen.tr/reform.htm).

### What is Sharia, and what does it mean in the United States?

Sharia is a broad term for Islamic guidance on how to live. It touches on prayer, fasting, charity, business dealings, and family life. Muslim legal scholars use the term *fiqh* for their work interpreting that guidance. Interpretations differ, and some governments have incorporated particular interpretations into their laws. In the U.S., a Muslim can fast during Ramadan or give zakat as part of practicing their faith. Federal and state law govern people's legal rights and obligations. The First Amendment protects religious exercise and bars the government from establishing a religion. [Qur'an 2:183–185](https://quran.com/2/183-185), [9:60](https://quran.com/9/60), [Introduction to the Islamic Tradition](https://www.usccb.org/committees/ecumenical-interreligious-affairs/introduction-islamic-tradition), and [U.S. Department of Justice on religious liberty](https://www.justice.gov/jm/1-15000-respect-religious-liberty-0).

### Does being Muslim imply a political agenda for the United States?

Muslim Americans have varied political views and a long history here. In 1831, Omar Ibn Said, a Muslim scholar enslaved in North Carolina, wrote his autobiography in Arabic. In Pew's 2017 survey of U.S. Muslims, 92% said they were proud to be American. Religious identity alone tells you little about a person's political aims. [Library of Congress manuscript and collection](https://www.loc.gov/collections/omar-ibn-said-collection/about-this-collection), [Pew's U.S. Muslim identity survey](https://www.pewresearch.org/religious-landscape-study/2017/07/26/identity-assimilation-and-community/), and [Pew's survey of political views](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2017/07/26/political-and-social-views/).

### What do Islamic sources say about child marriage?

The Qur'an gives no numerical minimum marriage age. Qur'an 4:6 refers to marriageable age and sound judgment in a passage about returning property to orphans. Some contemporary Muslim scholars draw on that verse to argue for maturity as a condition for marriage. Classical jurists also discussed marriage contracts involving minors and cited Qur'an 65:4, a verse about waiting periods after divorce. The traditional Sunni collection *Sahih al-Bukhari* contains reports about Aisha's young age at marriage.

Muslim scholars today differ on how the law should address child marriage. The International Islamic Fiqh Academy says authorities may regulate or prevent marriages of young girls to protect their welfare. The UK-based Ifta organisation recommends a minimum age of 18 even where civil law permits marriage earlier. [Qur'an 4:6](https://quran.com/4/6), [Ibn Kathir on 65:4](https://quran.com/65:4/tafsirs/en-tafisr-ibn-kathir), [Sahih al-Bukhari 5134](https://sunnah.com/bukhari:5134), [International Islamic Fiqh Academy resolution](https://iifa-aifi.org/en/6252.html), and [Ifta recommendation](https://www.ifta.org.uk/blog/child-marriage).

### What do U.S. Muslims say about violence against civilians?

In a 2017 Pew survey, 76% of U.S. Muslims said targeting and killing civilians to advance a political, social, or religious cause is never justified. The Qur'an calls on believers to act justly even when they dislike another group (5:8). [Pew survey on targeting civilians](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2017/07/26/terrorism-and-concerns-about-extremism/) and [Qur'an 5:8](https://quran.com/5/8).

### What does the Qur'an say about women's rights?

The Qur'an gives women a share of family inheritance and tells men to give the marriage gift directly to the bride. Inheritance shares depend on the family relationship and can differ by sex. The Qur'an also directs husbands to live with their wives honorably. How women experience those rights varies across families and societies. [Qur'an 4:4](https://quran.com/4/4), [4:7](https://quran.com/4/7), [4:11–12](https://quran.com/4/11-12), and [4:19](https://quran.com/4/19).

### Do all Muslim women wear hijab? What happens if they don't?

The Qur'an addresses modesty for men and women. Many Muslim women wear a headscarf, often called a hijab, as an expression of faith or modesty. Others wear it sometimes or never. Pew documented all of these practices among U.S. Muslim women. The modesty passage specifies no earthly penalty for going without a headscarf. Government rules and social pressure vary. A UN investigation found that Iranian authorities used violence to enforce the country's compulsory hijab policy. [Qur'an 24:30–31](https://quran.com/24/30-31), [Pew survey of U.S. Muslim religious practices](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2017/07/26/religious-beliefs-and-practices/), and [UN fact-finding report on Iran](https://digitallibrary.un.org/record/4084543/files/A_HRC_58_63-EN.pdf).

### Does Qur'an 4:34 give a husband the right to beat his wife?

Qur'an 4:34 addresses conflict between spouses. Many translations use the word "strike," and Muslim interpreters disagree on what the instruction permits. Some classical interpreters allowed a limited physical act and placed restrictions on it. Other Muslim interpreters argue for a nonviolent reading or emphasize that the Prophet's example rules out striking a spouse. Aisha reported that Muhammad never struck a woman, and the next verse calls for mediators when a marriage is at risk of breaking down. Egypt's Dar al-Ifta rejects using the verse to justify harming or humiliating a wife. Abuse is serious, and anyone experiencing it deserves safety and support. [Qur'an 4:34–35](https://quran.com/4/34-35), [Sahih Muslim 2328a](https://sunnah.com/muslim:2328a), [Dar al-Ifta's ruling](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/8168/my-husband-beats-me-and-says-that-it-is-his-right-does-islamic-law-back-him-up), and [Yaqeen discussion of interpretive differences](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/women-in-islamic-law-examining-five-prevalent-myths).

### What do Sunni and Shia mean?

Sunni and Shia are two major traditions within Islam. Both center belief in one God, the Qur'an, and Muhammad as God's messenger. Their historical division grew out of disputes over leadership after Muhammad's death. [Pew's background on Sunni and Shia traditions](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2012/08/09/the-worlds-muslims-unity-and-diversity-executive-summary/).
