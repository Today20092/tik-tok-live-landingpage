This series started with an unanswered du'a and ended somewhere I didn't expect. If the way I think about Allah affects my peace this much, is that worship? Is making du'a worship? And if worship covers so much, how am I supposed to do all of it?

## Du'a is worship

Allah says:

<QuranVerse verse="51:56" />

The next verse makes clear that this isn't because He needs anything from us:

<QuranVerse verse="51:57" />

And du'a is part of that worship. The Prophet ﷺ said:

<HadithQuote hadith="abudawud-1479-worship" />

That answered something from the first post. Even while I'm waiting for an answer, the asking itself is worship. It isn't wasted.

Allah doesn't gain anything from my worship. I'm the one who benefits: "Those who exert themselves do so for their own benefit––God does not need His creatures."[^benefit] The Quran also describes life as a test of which of us does best.[^test]

## More than sixty branches

But worship covers so much. It includes how I treat myself, other people, and Allah. It includes my actions, my thoughts, and how I see Him. How am I supposed to do all of it?

The Prophet ﷺ described faith as having many branches:

<HadithQuote hadith="muslim-35b-branches" />

What helped me is that this is a range, not a checklist I have to finish. It goes from the testimony of faith all the way down to moving something harmful out of someone's path.

When a man asked what he had to do, the Prophet ﷺ named the five daily prayers, fasting in Ramadan, and zakat. The man said he would do no more and no less, and the Prophet ﷺ said that if he was truthful, he would succeed.[^basics] In a hadith qudsi, Allah says the most beloved way to come closer to Him is through what He made obligatory, and then a person keeps coming closer through voluntary deeds.[^obligatory]

So the starting point is the obligations, especially the prayer on time.[^prayer] Then I grow from there, within my capacity. Allah does not burden any soul with more than it can bear.[^capacity] And the Prophet ﷺ warned that whoever overburdens himself in religion won't be able to keep it up.[^ease]

<HadithQuote hadith="bukhari-6464-consistent" showArabic={false} />

Small and consistent is better than a lot that I can't sustain.

## Is picking up trash worship?

I wanted a simple way to tell. What about removing a branch from the road? Picking up trash? Organizing the kitchen?

<HadithQuote hadith="bukhari-2472-branch" />

That's about as ordinary as a deed gets, and Allah forgave him for it.

The question that helps me is: **is this a ritual, or is this everyday life?** Rituals, like prayer and fasting, have to be done the way they were taught. We don't make up new ones.[^innovation] Lawful everyday things, like cleaning, eating, working, or helping someone, aren't rituals. What can turn them into worship is the intention behind them.

<HadithQuote hadith="bukhari-1" />

The Prophet ﷺ even said a person is rewarded for what he spends for Allah's sake, even a morsel of food he puts in his wife's mouth.[^morsel]

So the time between the five prayers is still part of my religion. The prayers have fixed times, and I can bring an intention to what I do in between.

## The best intention: seeking His Face

Then I asked: what's the best intention to have? Is there a statement I should say?

From what I've learned, intention is in the heart. There's no required wording. What I keep finding in the Quran is praise for people who act seeking the Face of Allah, and nothing else. People who feed the poor say, "We feed you for the sake of God alone: We seek neither recompense nor thanks from you."[^feed] Another verse describes someone who gives not to repay a favor, "but for the sake of his Lord the Most High."[^sake] Several of these verses use the Arabic phrase for seeking His Face, *wajh*.[^face-verses]

## What does "His Face" mean?

This was my last question. If I wake up and say, "I seek Your Face, O Allah, and Your pleasure," what am I actually saying?

First, what I'm *not* saying. I'm not picturing anything. Allah says:

<QuranVerse verse="55:26" />
<QuranVerse verse="55:27" />

And He says there is nothing like Him.[^like] So I affirm what He said about Himself without imagining how.

What I *am* saying is that I want Allah Himself. I want more than the reward, and I don't want the praise of people. Everything else perishes. What remains is the Face of my Lord. Seeking His Face means aiming my deeds at the One who remains.

The Quran also describes where this leads. It describes faces on the Day of Judgment, "radiant faces, looking towards their Lord."[^radiant] The Prophet ﷺ said that the people of Paradise will see their Lord, and that nothing they were given will be dearer to them than that.[^sight]

## Where this left me

I started this whole conversation frustrated that my du'a hadn't been answered. I ended up asking what worship even is.

The asking is worship. The waiting is a test of what I think about Allah. And the ordinary things I do between prayers can all be aimed at the same place: seeking His Face.

I still want what I asked for. But I understand better now that the asking was never wasted.
