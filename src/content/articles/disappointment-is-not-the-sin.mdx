After learning about the hadith on impatience in du'a, I had a question I couldn't let go of. What do I do with the disappointment?

When I want something and it doesn't show up, I feel let down. Can I prevent that feeling? Is the feeling itself the problem? Or would trusting Allah mean I'm just not disappointed anymore, because I know He has better to give?

## The feeling isn't what the hadith forbids

When the Prophet's son Ibrahim was dying, the Prophet ﷺ wept. Abdur Rahman ibn Auf asked, "even you are weeping!" and he said, "this is mercy." Then he said:

<HadithQuote hadith="bukhari-1303-ibrahim" />

That helped me a lot. Tears and grief aren't a lack of faith. The Prophet ﷺ felt both. What he controlled was what he *said*: only what pleases our Lord.

So the sadness itself isn't the sin. What I need to watch is the verdict I reach about Allah because of it: that He didn't hear me, that He let me down, or that His handling of my life isn't good.

## Why the verdict matters more

This hadith made it click for me:

<HadithQuote hadith="bukhari-7405-good-opinion" />

How I think about Allah matters. If I let disappointment turn into "Allah doesn't care about me," it's no longer only a mood. It's a belief about Allah.

The verse I keep coming back to is this one:

<QuranVerse verse="2:216" />

The verse ends, "God knows and you do not."[^knowledge] I want things that might not be good for me because I can't see the future. Wanting something tells me what I'm drawn to. It doesn't tell me whether it's good for me.

I wrote about this before, about catching myself thinking I know better than Allah.

<ArticleLink article="arrogance-and-trusting-allah" />

## Ya'qub, and still wanting it

What if the door stays closed and my heart still wants it? Is that a fault?

I think of Ya'qub, peace be upon him. He lost Yusuf for years and never stopped grieving. But look at where he took that grief:

<QuranVerse verse="12:86" />

He complained *to* Allah, not *about* Allah. And in the very next verse he tells his sons to go and search, and not to despair of God's mercy.[^yaqub] He still wanted Yusuf back, and he still hoped.

That's what I mean by releasing the outcome while continuing to ask. I keep asking. What I let go of is demanding that it happen on my timeline, in the form I picked.

## Gratitude while still asking

This is where I really struggle. When I want something and it doesn't show up, it becomes really hard for me to see any good that I already have. The missing thing takes up the whole picture.

But asking and being grateful aren't opposites. The Prophet ﷺ described the believer's affairs as all good: when something good happens, he is grateful, and when something hard happens, he is patient, and both are good for him.[^believer]

Allah also says, "If you are thankful, I will give you more."[^thankful] And He tells the Prophet ﷺ to speak about the blessings of his Lord.[^blessings]

One practice came out of this conversation. Before making my request, I name a few things I already have and thank Allah for them. It isn't a ritual from the Sunnah. It's a way of not letting the one thing I'm missing hide everything else.

## So what is contentment?

I asked what contentment actually feels like. Here's how I understand it now.

It's not the absence of wanting. Ya'qub still wanted Yusuf. It's not the absence of sadness. The Prophet ﷺ wept for his son.

I think it's peace with *who is deciding*. If Allah is the All-Knowing and the Most Merciful, then what He decrees for me comes from knowledge and mercy, even when I can't see how. I can't force that feeling. But the more I learn about who Allah is, the easier that peace comes.

That raised another question. If the way I think about Allah matters this much, is that itself worship?
