Assalamu alaikum. I'm Ayoub, and I started these live sessions because I wanted a place where people could ask about Islam without being made to feel stupid.

## What made me want to go live

When I watched religious discussions on TikTok, I sometimes saw Muslim hosts invite Christians, Hindus, and people of other faiths into conversations that became uncomfortable to watch. A guest would get caught out by a question, and their embarrassment seemed to become part of the entertainment.

I can't know what was in anyone's heart. But what I saw troubled me. I worried about what someone unfamiliar with Islam would take away from it, and whether the person asking questions had actually learned anything.

I kept thinking about the way Allah, subhanahu wa ta'ala, tells us to invite people to Him. [Quran 16:125](https://quran.com/16/125) calls for wisdom, good advice, and discussion in the best manner. That's the standard I want to hold myself to when I speak about Islam.

So I started reading the Quran live. I wanted us to open it, read what it says, and take people's questions seriously. Someone who comes with a sincere question deserves patience, even when we disagree.

## I'm learning too

These sessions also give me a reason to keep studying. I've started reading *Riyad as-Salihin*, often translated as *The Meadows of the Righteous*, live as well.

If you'd like to read along, [download the abridged and annotated English PDF of Riyad as-Salihin](https://go.alphabravomedia.co/riyad-as-salign-pdf).

Imam al-Nawawi's [collection](https://sunnah.com/riyadussalihin) brings together hadith on belief, worship, character, and everyday conduct, with Quranic passages throughout its chapters. I want to study these teachings with scholarly explanations, rather than treat my first impression as the answer.

My goal is to help people understand the fundamentals of Islam while continuing to learn myself. When a question goes beyond what I understand, I need to take it to someone with the knowledge to answer it properly.

## Why this website exists

During a live session, I'll mention a book, a place to read a translation, or another resource that helps answer a question. Those references can be hard to find once the live ends.

I made this website so the link in my TikTok bio could take you to one place with the resources we discuss. You can find the books I'm reading, Quran translations, recordings, and links to keep learning at your own pace. Where providers offer free copies, the links explain their eligibility rules.

Whether you're Muslim, new to Islam, or exploring another faith, you're welcome to read along and ask questions.

## Putting what we learn into practice

Being Muslim doesn't mean every difficulty disappears or that we have everything figured out. We still need to learn, and we need to let that learning affect how we live.

I think of this as time spent learning, time spent sharing what we understand, and time spent applying it. Those parts overlap. I don't want to become so busy explaining something to other people that I forget to work on it myself.

[Quran 61:2 and 61:3](https://quran.com/61/2-3) warn believers against saying what they do not do. I take that as a reminder to examine my own actions before becoming comfortable with giving advice.

There's also a story I remembered about a preacher who delayed speaking about freeing slaves until he had freed one himself. [Shaykh Abdurragmaan Khan recounts it on SeekersGuidance](https://seekersguidance.org/articles/general-artices/masters-and-millennials-acting-upon-ones-knowledge/), attributing it to Hasan al-Basri. In that account, he waited several Fridays until he could afford to buy and free an enslaved person, then encouraged others to free theirs.

This is a story attributed to Hasan al-Basri, not a hadith of the Prophet ﷺ. I haven't verified its chain of transmission, so I wouldn't use it as proof of a religious ruling. What stayed with me was the reminder to take my own advice seriously.

I don't mean that we have to become perfect before encouraging anyone else to do good. I mean that I need to acknowledge my shortcomings and keep working on them. I hope these sessions help me stay accountable, and that we can remind one another with kindness.

## What I hope to do next

I'm planning a podcast where I can bring together questions from the month and discuss them with my local imam or other guests with sound Islamic knowledge. I want people to have time to hear an explanation, ask follow-up questions, and understand where an answer comes from.

For now, I'll keep reading, sharing the resources we use, and working on what I learn. May Allah keep my intentions sincere and help us put beneficial knowledge into practice.

Ayoub
