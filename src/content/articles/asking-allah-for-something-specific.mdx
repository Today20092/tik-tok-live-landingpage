In my last post, I wrote about the hadith on unanswered du'a and the warning against giving up. That left me with a more personal question.

Let's say you meet someone, you like them, and you want to marry them. You make du'a for some time, and nothing seems to be working. Do you stop asking even though you still want it? Or do you change your du'a? When do you change it?

And underneath that: should I even be asking for something specific? Maybe it's safer to ask in general terms, so I'm not let down when the specific thing doesn't happen.

## The Quran is full of specific requests

My instinct was that asking in general terms was the more humble or safer choice. But when I looked, the prophets asked for very specific things.

Zakariyya, peace be upon him, asked for a child when he was old and his wife was unable to have children:

<QuranVerse verse="21:89" />

The Quran describes his prayer in more than one place, in detail. In Surah Maryam he describes his weak bones and grey hair and asks for an heir.[^zakariyya] And Allah answered him with Yahya.[^answered]

So asking for a specific thing isn't a lack of trust. It's something the prophets did.

## The general form is there too

At the same time, the Quran also teaches general du'as:

<QuranVerse verse="2:201" />

There's also the du'a for joy in our spouses and children, which asks for the good of marriage without naming a person.[^spouses]

And there's Musa, peace be upon him. After he watered the flocks for the two women at the well, he withdrew into the shade and prayed:[^musa]

<QuranVerse verse="28:24" />

He named his need, but not the form it had to take.

So I don't think I have to choose. I can ask for the specific thing *and* for whatever is good for me.

## What istikhara adds

This is where istikhara helps me. In the istikhara supplication, you ask Allah to make the matter easy if it's good for your religion, your life, and your outcome, and to turn it away if it's bad. Then you ask for good wherever it is, and to be content with it:

<HadithQuote hadith="bukhari-1166-contentment" showArabic={false} />

That last part is the hardest for me. It's one thing to ask for good wherever it is. It's another to ask to be *content* with it when it's not what I pictured.

## Two du'as instead of one

What I took from this conversation is that I can keep two du'as going at the same time.

1. **The specific one.** "O Allah, if this person is good for my religion and my worldly life, make it easy for me."
2. **The general one.** "And if not, turn it away from me and me away from it, give me what is good wherever it is, and make me content with it."

Those words follow the meaning of the istikhara supplication. They aren't a fixed formula.

## So when do I change the du'a?

Not just because time has passed and nothing seems to be happening. From the hadith in the last post, turning "it's not happening" into "Allah has refused me" is exactly the conclusion I'm supposed to avoid.

I think the du'a changes when the situation is decided. The person marries someone else. They clearly say no. The door actually closes. At that point, asking for that specific person makes less sense, and the general du'a carries on: give me what's good, and make me content with it.

## Stopping because I trust, not because I'm resentful

That raised another question for me. Even when I stop asking for a specific thing, there's a difference in *why* I stop.

One person stops because they've concluded, "I asked and asked, and He didn't give it to me." That's the conclusion the hadith warns against.

Another person stops because the matter has been decided, and they're placing it with Allah. They might still want it. The desire doesn't have to disappear. But they aren't keeping score against Allah on their own timeline.

The words might look the same from the outside. The difference is what's in the heart when you stop.
